Quarterly Planning for Small Teams

By Jordan Reyes · March 2026 · 6 min read

Most planning advice is written for companies with program managers, steering committees, and a budget for off-sites. Small teams need something lighter: a cadence that produces a short list of commitments without consuming a week of calendar time.

This article describes the three-meeting structure we have used for two years across product and platform work. It optimizes for one thing: every commitment has an owner and an explicit "stop doing" counterpart.

The three meetings

The whole cycle fits in five working days and three meetings, each with a fixed agenda and a hard stop.

MeetingLengthOutput
Retrospective60 minThree lessons, written
Options review90 minRanked candidate list
Commitment45 minFinal list with owners

Writing the options memo

Before the options review, one person writes a two-page memo listing every candidate project with a one-paragraph case. The discipline of the memo matters more than the meeting itself; half the candidates usually eliminate themselves on paper.

Common failure modes

Three patterns account for most failed quarters:

  • Carry-over creep. Unfinished work re-enters the list without re-justification.
  • Owner inflation. A commitment with two owners has no owner.
  • Silent scope growth. The "stop doing" list is ignored after week two.

The fix for all three is the same: the written memo is the source of truth, and anything not in it does not exist.

What to measure

Track only two numbers per quarter: the fraction of commitments delivered as written, and the number of unplanned projects that displaced planned ones. Both are countable in an afternoon and resist gaming better than velocity-style metrics.
